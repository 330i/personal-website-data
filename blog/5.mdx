A bit of an odd thing my brain was bugging me to write down.
While I was looking into the AUX to AVC-LAN project<HoverTextBlock>Which I had no time to work on at all.</HoverTextBlock>, I saw that Toyota Corollas and Matrixes (Matrices?) came with a JBL system.
And then I noticed that the head units for the LE and S trim had RDS<HoverTextBlock>Radio Data System. Something that displays radio station data.</HoverTextBlock> written on it.

When I went deeper into that rabbit hole, I found that the North American Corolla came with 5 different head units during that time.
That includes the MY2003-2004 CD/Casette and CD-only, MY2005-2008 CD-only with and without RDS, and the rare MY2005-2008 JBL head unit.

The MY2002-2003 Toyota Voltz came with a blanking plate<HoverTextBlock>It didn't come with a radio from factory. Apparently blanking plates were a common thing in Japan. A dealer-supplied or aftermarket head unit would be installed after delivery. The car came with <a href="https://en.japanclassic.ru/booklets/852-toyota-voltz-2003-zze136-zze138.html" target="_blank">5 different dealer-installed head units</a>. Two of which are available in the E120.</HoverTextBlock>.

So that's like 16 different head units. 9 of which was made to fit the curved dashboard trim piece.

An interesting food for thought from back when cars were more regionalized.
